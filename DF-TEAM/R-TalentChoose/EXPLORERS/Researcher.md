# 🔦Researcher

**Experience requirement?:** 

*No. But can be useful.*

---

### Researcher — Who is it?

A Researcher is a person and a way to contribute through practice.

Someone who takes a question, hypothesis, problem or unexplored possibility and finds out what actually happens.

It can be small or enormous. **The size does not decide the value.** What matters is whether the research provides useful information that was not already known or documented.

### What can you research?

Your own question. An Idea-creator's hypothesis. A public Issue or Discussion. Another contributor's experiment. Even a relevant question from outside DF-SHE.

You may work independently or together with others.

Reproducing an existing result alone is not enough. A changed condition, environment or independent observation can make that reproduction valuable.

### Results do not have to be positive

Something worked. Something worked only under certain conditions. Something was ineffective. Something failed. Something unexpected happened.

A result that disproves an approach can be just as useful as one that confirms it — if it provides information that was not already known or documented.

### How small can it be?

One reproducible experiment can be enough.

A large investigation can become a long-term contribution.

You do not need a laboratory or a perfect research environment. You need something that can be examined, reproduced or observed.

### What should be recorded?

At minimum, another person should understand:

- **Conditions** — what was tested and under what circumstances.
- **Method** — what was applied, changed or examined.
- **Result** — what actually happened.
- **Conclusion** — what the result tells us.

Sources, measurements, comparisons and technical details can strengthen the research.

### Different fields are welcome

Your background does not have to be programming.

A mathematician, biologist, accountant or someone from another field may bring knowledge that becomes useful when examining a real question, comparing an approach or testing a possibility inside the project.

### Programming and AI

Programming can make practical research easier, especially when working directly with code, but it is not the only path.

AI tools can help you inspect code, reproduce experiments and learn while researching. They are tools, not the Researcher's replacement: the result must still be understood, checked and confirmed by the Researcher.

### Researcher and Idea-creator

**Idea-creator — theorist.**  
Creates hypotheses and theories, proposes concepts and explores what could exist.

**Researcher — practitioner.**  
Takes questions into practice, examines what happens and brings back evidence.

They can work together or independently. You can be either, or both.

### Your research becomes part of the history

A confirmed result can become part of your contributor history.

Whenever possible, the record should lead back to the original Issue, Discussion or other source that started the research, so others can see what was actually investigated.

### How to contribute

Choose something worth investigating. Define what you want to find out. Set reproducible conditions and a clear method. Run the investigation. Record what happened, including unexpected results. Then publish it through the appropriate DF-SHE channel.
